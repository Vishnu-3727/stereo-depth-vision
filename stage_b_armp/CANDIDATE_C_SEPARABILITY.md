# Candidate C Separability Analysis — Supervision Coverage vs FT3D Pretraining/Initialization as the Explanation of the ARM-P Delta

Zero-GPU analysis document. No training was run, no evaluation was run, no experiment was launched or prepared, no hyperparameter was tuned, no new metric was invented, and no existing file was modified for this document. ARM-P is treated throughout as an INITIALIZATION INTERVENTION only. CONTROL is RANDOM INITIALIZATION under the original P2A recipe, never P2A checkpoint initialization. The three seeds are reported separately and are never pooled. The 0.0444 px figure is a historical run-to-run spread quoted as context only, never as a significance threshold. No significance claim and no confidence interval appears in this document. No mechanism is inferred from disparity-bin improvements alone.

## 1. QUESTION

The frozen P2A platform shows a repeated directional observation: the ARM-P arm (initialized from the Stage-1 FT3D-pretrained checkpoint of itself, then KITTI fine-tuned under the exact P2A recipe) records lower frozen-contract KITTI error than the CONTROL arm (random initialization, same recipe) on best and final checkpoints at each of three seeds, per `stage_b_armp/ARMP_CLOSURE_RECORD.md` Sections 4–7. The recipe, architecture, loss, augmentation, optimizer, scheduler, dataset split, seed handling, and evaluation contract are identical between arms within each run; the only intended independent variable is weight initialization.

Two competing explanations of that delta are on the table:

- **Explanation A (initialization/pretraining):** the delta comes from FT3D pretraining as initialization — i.e., from starting KITTI fine-tuning at the Stage-1 weight point, with everything that point bundles (weight scale, feature statistics, optimizer trajectory position). This is the ARM-P intervention as preregistered.
- **Explanation C (supervision coverage):** the delta comes from supervision coverage — i.e., from ARM-P having seen far more high-disparity supervised pixels (FT3D staged [64,128) mass ~1.23 billion vs ~559,057 raw KITTI training pixels, ~2,200x, per `stage_b_armp/20260918T053135Z/README.md`) while the control never saw them, so that any initialization carrying that exposure would show the same delta regardless of other pretraining content.

The separability question is: can supervision coverage be varied independently of FT3D pretraining/initialization on the frozen P2A platform, so that a matched-coverage experiment could attribute the ARM-P delta to C rather than A? If yes, Candidate C is IDENTIFIABLE and a specification follows. If no clean manipulation exists, Candidate C is NOT IDENTIFIABLE and no experiment is manufactured.

## 2. STAGE-A MOTIVATION

Stage A closed with `architecture_gate = NO ARCHITECTURE JUSTIFIED` in `stage_a_diagnostics/mechanism_verdict.json`, whose `gate_rationale` states explicitly: "Largest single identified effect (supervision coverage ~2x under at GT>=96) is a DATA fix, not an architecture change (high_disparity.json:supervision_coverage)". The supporting mechanism record (`mechanism_verdict.json` mechanism id 6, label SUPPORTED, name "Training / supervision distribution") records effective training mass at GT>=96 after P2A augmentation of 0.444% versus eval 0.898% — still ~2x under-covered (3.9x raw before augmentation) — with the counter-evidence that it is a DATA property (weighs toward a data/augmentation fix, never an architecture candidate) and that supervision alone does not explain the ~31% sub-64px gap where coverage is adequate.

The FT3D coverage evidence comes from the Stage-B pre-flight audit `stage_b_armp/20260918T053135Z/README.md` verdict 5 and its backing `training_distribution_audit.json`: per-bin FT3D_frac/KITTI_eval_frac at [64,80)/[80,96)/[96,112)/[112,128) = 2.02 / 7.66 / 5.78 / 4.70, with estimated staged FT3D pixels in [64,128) ~1.23 BILLION vs ~559,057 raw KITTI training pixels (~2,200x), holding after >=184 masking (masked-away FT3D fraction >= 184 px = 0.00446, negligible). The same audit records the corpus limits that matter for any manipulation: FT3D available subset is A+C only, 14,460 usable triplets, B entirely missing, monkaa empty, and upstream checkpoint provenance NOT ESTABLISHED.

The platform facts that constrain every candidate manipulation: the model has 24 disparity candidates, 3 downsample levels, feature stride 8, max_disparity_px 184 (`stage_b_armp/20260918T053135Z/README.md` P2A frozen reference; `stage_b_armp/20260919T012646Z_tier2_seed1/scripts/finetune_pilot.py` `ARM_V_CONFIG`); the loss is `masked_smooth_l1` with `valid = target > 0` and additionally `target < max_disparity` when given (`src/losses/disparity.py` lines 27–45); and ARM-P got its high-disparity exposure during PRETRAINING, not during KITTI fine-tuning, while the control never got it at all (`stage_b_armp/ARMP_CLOSURE_RECORD.md` Sections 2 and 13a–13b). Any separability design must reckon with that temporal and corpus asymmetry.

## 3. CAUSAL VARIABLES

Operational definitions on THIS codebase (`stage_b_armp/20260919T012646Z_tier2_seed1/scripts/finetune_pilot.py` as the frozen recipe; `src/losses/disparity.py` as the frozen loss):

- **Initialization/pretraining:** the weight tensor loaded before optimizer/scheduler construction. CONTROL = PyTorch-default random init via fresh `StereoNet(config)` construction (`finetune_pilot.py:222-223`, `apply_init` no-op for `"random"`, `finetune_pilot.py:185-201`). ARM-P = strict `model.load_state_dict(sd, strict=True)` of the `["model"]` weights from `stage_b_armp/20260918T062146Z_stage1_pretrain/checkpoints/armp_stage1_best.pth` (sha256 recorded in `stage_b_armp/ARMP_CLOSURE_RECORD.md` Section 2), applied after the integrity guard and before Adam/CosineAnnealingLR construction (`finetune_pilot.py:226-241`). No optimizer state is inherited.
- **Supervision coverage:** the disparity histogram the loss actually sees during training — i.e., the distribution of valid GT pixels (`target > 0` and `target < max_disparity`) presented to `masked_smooth_l1` (`finetune_pilot.py:299`; `src/losses/disparity.py:43-45`) over the full fine-tuning run. This is an exposure quantity (which GT values supply gradients, how often), not a weight value.
- **Augmentation/scale sampling:** the `ScaledCroppedKitti.__getitem__` procedure (`finetune_pilot.py:88-123`): per-sample `s = exp(uniform(log 0.7, log 1.7))`, window `w = round(512/s)`, `h = round(256/s)`, uniform random crop, resize back to 512x256 (LINEAR images, NEAREST disparity), then `disp_c[disp_c > 0] *= sx` with `sx = 512/w` (the REALISED horizontal factor), then per-image multiplicative intensity jitter sigma 0.1. Scale sampling and disparity rescaling are one fused procedure in this code.
- **Dataset/domain:** which image/disparity population the samples come from (KITTI `hailo_calib` 160 scenes for training vs FT3D A+C 14,460 triplets vs any mixture), including scene content, texture, occlusion structure, and native disparity distribution before augmentation.
- **Architecture:** the P2A `StereoNet` graph and constants (397,954 params, 70 keys, 24 candidates, 3 downsample levels, shift right, regression_normalize true, no BatchNorm, max_disparity_px 184). Frozen by rule; this document proposes no change to cost volume, grouping, correlation, aggregation, refinement, or readout.
- **Loss:** the function `masked_smooth_l1(prediction, target, max_disparity=None, beta=1.0)` (`src/losses/disparity.py:21-51`) called with `beta=1.0` and `max_disparity=float(config.max_disparity_px)` (`finetune_pilot.py:299`). The function form (smooth-L1 over valid pixels, mean reduction) versus its masking argument value are distinguished below.

Explicit conflation warning (the crux): **on KITTI fine-tuning, supervision coverage and augmentation/scale sampling are the SAME KNOB.** The disparity histogram the loss sees is produced BY the scale sampler, because `disp_c[disp_c > 0] *= sx` multiplies GT disparities by the realised horizontal factor. Sampling a larger `s` yields a smaller crop window `w`, a larger `sx = 512/w`, and therefore larger presented disparities that push pixels into higher bins. There is no independent coverage dial on the KITTI path: every change to presented high-disparity mass via this route IS a change to the augmentation distribution (what image scales, resolutions, and contexts the network sees). This document treats them as one fused knob wherever the KITTI-time route is concerned and says so in each route assessment rather than letting them conflate implicitly.

## 4. IDENTIFIABILITY TEST

The test: does there exist a matched-coverage intervention that changes supervision exposure while holding initialization AND every other Section 3 variable fixed? Every candidate route constructible on this codebase is enumerated. Each states exactly which variables move and which stay fixed. Verdict per route first, reasoning after.

**Route 1 — Widen/alter the KITTI scale range (e.g., raise SCALE_HI above 1.7, or reshape the log-uniform). Verdict: FAILS — moves augmentation/scale sampling by construction.**
Changing `SCALE_LO`/`SCALE_HI` (`finetune_pilot.py:69`) or the sampling law (`finetune_pilot.py:91`) is definitionally an augmentation change: it alters the image-scale, crop-size, and context distribution every gradient step sees, plus the intensity-jitter interaction stays while geometry shifts underneath it. Because of the same-knob identity (Section 3), the coverage change cannot be credited to coverage rather than to augmented image statistics. Additionally the knob saturates adversarially: pushing `sx` higher drives presented disparities toward and beyond `max_disparity_px = 184`, where the frozen loss call (`finetune_pilot.py:299` with `max_disparity=184.0`) EXCLUDES them (`src/losses/disparity.py:45`), so extreme upscaling removes supervision instead of adding it. Initialization, dataset source, architecture, and loss form stay fixed; augmentation/scale sampling moves, so the intervention is confounded with explanation C2 (augmentation effect) by design.

**Route 2 — Reweight or bias crop selection toward high-disparity KITTI regions (e.g., accept/reject crops by GT content, or sample (y,x) non-uniformly). Verdict: FAILS — moves dataset/domain composition and sampling dynamics.**
The crop position draw (`finetune_pilot.py:97-98`, uniform over valid top-left positions) and the uniform per-sample draw are part of the frozen sampling rule. Biasing them changes which scenes, depths, and occlusion structures supply gradients — a dataset-composition change even with the same underlying 160 scenes — and changes the effective epoch (revisits of rare near-field pixels) and gradient variance. Total addable mass is capped by the raw KITTI stock (~559,057 pixels in [64,128)): reaching anywhere near FT3D-scale mass requires massive repetition of a tiny pixel stock, which changes optimization dynamics (memorization of rare crops) rather than matching FT3D exposure. Initialization, architecture, loss form, and nominal scale range stay fixed; dataset/domain sampling and augmentation procedure move.

**Route 3 — Disparity-mask the loss via the existing `max_disparity` argument (e.g., a control arm with lowered `max_disparity`, or staged masking schedules). Verdict: FAILS — can only REMOVE supervision, never add the missing FT3D mass; and any added schedule is a loss-call change.**
On the masking-vs-loss question stated explicitly: using the already-exposed `max_disparity` parameter (`src/losses/disparity.py:24,44-45`) without editing the function body is NOT a loss-function change — the function form, beta, and reduction are untouched; it is a supervision-coverage change implemented through the loss's own masking argument. That distinction is creditable but useless here, because masking is subtractive only. The control is already UNDER-covered at GT>=96 (0.444% effective vs 0.898% eval); removing further mass (e.g., excluding GT>=96 from the control, or lowering `max_disparity`) widens the gap it is meant to close and cannot synthesize the ~1.23 billion FT3D pixels the control never saw. A design that instead masks ARM-P down to control-level coverage inverts the question (it tests degradation under removal, not whether added coverage explains the delta) and still changes the per-batch valid-pixel count `n` returned alongside the loss (`src/losses/disparity.py:46-51`), altering gradient noise. Initialization, augmentation, dataset, and architecture stay fixed; coverage moves in the wrong direction, so the route cannot match FT3D exposure.

**Route 4 — Add FT3D (or any other high-disparity data) into KITTI fine-tuning (joint training, mixed batches, or FT3D warm-start of the control). Verdict: FAILS — moves dataset/domain and curriculum, and redefines the initialization comparison.**
ARM-P's exposure was sequential (20-epoch FT3D pretraining producing a weight point, then 200-epoch KITTI fine-tuning from that point) while the control had zero FT3D steps. Joint FT3D+KITTI fine-tuning of a random-init control is a different curriculum (different gradient sequence, different forgetting dynamics, different domain mixture per batch) on a different dataset, not the same recipe with one knob turned. It also collides with the corpus facts: the available FT3D subset is A+C only with B wholly missing (`stage_b_armp/20260918T053135Z/README.md` verdict 3), so "matched" FT3D exposure cannot even be matched to full SceneFlow. Initialization nominally stays random at step 0, but dataset/domain moves and the training-time distribution moves, so any delta against the original control is attributable to D (domain/data-composition effect), not isolated to C.

**Route 5 — Alter the pretraining corpus itself (e.g., pretrain a control-init on low-disparity-only FT3D, or on KITTI itself, and compare against full-FT3D ARM-P). Verdict: FAILS — moves initialization by construction; coverage and initialization are the same knob at pretraining time.**
Any change to what the Stage-1 run saw IS a change to the resulting weight point that `apply_init` strict-loads (`finetune_pilot.py:191-201`). There is no way to hold "initialization" fixed while varying "pretraining coverage", because pretraining coverage is fully absorbed into the initialization tensor before KITTI fine-tuning begins. A low-disp-pretrained control versus full ARM-P would differ in weight scale, feature statistics, and trajectory position — the entire A bundle (`stage_b_armp/ARMP_CLOSURE_RECORD.md` Section 12: the intervention bundles SceneFlow pretraining with everything that comes with it). KITTI-time augmentation, dataset, architecture, and loss could stay fixed, but the variable to be held fixed (initialization) is the one that moves.

**Route 6 — Loss reweighting of high-disparity pixels (per-pixel weights, bin-weighted loss) without changing sampling. Verdict: FAILS — moves loss (requires changing the frozen loss call into a new function) and is not exposure matching.**
`masked_smooth_l1` has no per-pixel weight argument (`src/losses/disparity.py:21-26`); upweighting GT>=96 requires a new loss function, violating the frozen-loss requirement and confounding C with a loss effect. Moreover, scaling gradients on rare pixels is not the same causal quantity as presenting billions of diverse high-disparity pixels across scenes; it changes gradient magnitude without changing scene/texture/occlusion diversity, optimizer trajectory, or representation formation. Initialization, augmentation, and dataset stay fixed; loss moves and coverage-as-exposure does not actually match.

No further route is constructible without violating the frozen platform (architecture change), the frozen recipe (optimizer/scheduler/epoch change — which would move the trajectory variable), or the task prohibitions (new metric, hyperparameter tuning, True GWC, another ARM-P seed). Every route that adds high-disparity exposure moves augmentation/scale sampling, dataset/domain, loss, or initialization itself — and the one route that holds all of those fixed (subtractive masking) moves coverage in the wrong direction. The identifiability test therefore FAILS: there is no matched-coverage intervention on the frozen P2A platform that changes supervision exposure while holding initialization and every other listed variable fixed.

## 6. NECESSARY CONTROLS

Stated as the minimum control set that WOULD be required to distinguish the four effects, so that the reason no subset suffices is on the record. No control is launched by this document.

- **A (coverage effect):** two arms sharing random initialization, identical recipe, identical augmentation, identical dataset, identical loss, differing ONLY in presented high-disparity mass. Sections 4 Routes 1–3 show no such pair exists without moving augmentation or removing rather than adding mass.
- **B (pretraining/initialization effect):** the already-completed CONTROL-vs-ARM-P comparison (`stage_b_armp/ARMP_CLOSURE_RECORD.md` Sections 2–7): random versus Stage-1 strict-loaded init, everything else identical. This control isolates initialization as a bundle but deliberately does NOT separate coverage-within-pretraining from other pretraining content — that separation is exactly what fails in Route 5.
- **C (augmentation/scale-sampling effect):** two random-init arms differing only in the scale sampler with coverage consequences measured and reported as augmentation, not coverage. Any Route 1 arm IS this control, not a coverage arm: it belongs to the augmentation explanation by construction.
- **D (domain/data-composition effect):** two random-init arms differing only in training population (e.g., KITTI-only vs KITTI+FT3D-A+C joint) with the domain mixture as the declared intervention. Any Route 4 arm IS this control, not a coverage arm.

Because every feasible coverage-adding arm collapses into C or D (or moves B via Route 5, or the loss via Route 6), the four-way distinction cannot be instantiated. Listing the controls does not create an experiment; it records why the experiment cannot be built.

## 7. CONFOUND AUDIT

For each Route 4 manipulation: what changes, what must remain invariant, and how invariance would be verified — stated so the failure is auditable, not asserted.

- **Route 1 (scale-range change):** Changes: presented disparity histogram; image-scale/crop-size/context distribution; fraction of pixels pushed beyond `max_disparity_px = 184` into the loss-excluded regime. Must remain invariant for a pure-C claim: augmentation distribution, valid-pixel noise profile, and image statistics. Verification: log per-epoch presented-disparity histogram AND per-epoch image-scale/crop-size histogram from `ScaledCroppedKitti.draws` provenance (`finetune_pilot.py:83,117-118,339-349`) plus valid-pixel counts `n` per batch (`src/losses/disparity.py:46`); invariance FAILS on inspection because the same sampler produces both histograms — the audit would show them moving together, confirming the same-knob identity.
- **Route 2 (crop biasing):** Changes: scene/region sampling frequencies, revisit rate of rare near-field pixels, effective epoch length, gradient variance. Must remain invariant: dataset composition and sampling dynamics. Verification: per-scene sample counts and per-pixel revisit histograms from the loader; invariance FAILS because high-disp KITTI mass is spatially concentrated and scarce (~559,057 raw pixels), so any material coverage gain appears as extreme revisit skew.
- **Route 3 (disparity masking):** Changes: valid-pixel count and high-bin mass (downward only). Must remain invariant: total exposure opportunity and gradient-noise profile. Verification: valid-pixel counts and bin masses per epoch; invariance FAILS in direction (mass decreases) and in noise (counts drop), and no setting adds mass.
- **Route 4 (FT3D mixed into fine-tuning):** Changes: training population, domain mixture, curriculum order (joint vs sequential), forgetting dynamics. Must remain invariant: dataset, recipe, and trajectory structure. Verification: dataset manifests, batch-composition logs, and the Stage-1-then-fine-tune vs joint-training schedule comparison; invariance FAILS because joint training is a different causal path from pretrain-then-fine-tune even at matched pixel counts, and the FT3D subset itself is A+C-only with B missing.
- **Route 5 (pretraining-corpus change):** Changes: the Stage-1 weight point itself (scale, statistics, trajectory). Must remain invariant: initialization. Verification: checkpoint sha256 and weight-statistics comparison against `3ae6fb3be6b2bda9287b325ccbe6d1397744c8f20ef5e56c046176d9f1a29be7`; invariance FAILS by construction — different corpus means different bytes.
- **Route 6 (loss reweighting):** Changes: loss function and gradient magnitudes on rare pixels. Must remain invariant: loss form. Verification: `recipe_diff`-style code comparison against `src/losses/disparity.py` plus per-bin gradient-norm logs; invariance FAILS because the function must be edited to accept weights.

## 9. STOP CONDITION

The stop condition is MET. Section 4 establishes that supervision coverage cannot be manipulated independently of another causal variable on the frozen P2A platform: KITTI-time coverage IS the scale-sampler output (same knob, `finetune_pilot.py:88-123` with `disp_c[disp_c > 0] *= sx`); subtractive loss masking (`src/losses/disparity.py:24,44-45`) can only remove the already-scarce high-disparity mass; additive routes move augmentation (Route 1), dataset/domain and curriculum (Routes 2, 4), the loss function (Route 6), or initialization itself (Route 5). Because coverage cannot be manipulated independently, Candidate C is declared NOT IDENTIFIABLE. No experiment is manufactured, no strained proxy is substituted, and Section 5 is NOT APPLICABLE by rule.
