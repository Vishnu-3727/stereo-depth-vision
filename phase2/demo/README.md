# Stereo depth demonstration — how far away is everything?

Written for a reader who does not work on stereo vision. No maths is required
to follow it. Every claim is tagged **MEASURED**, **DERIVED**, **INFERRED** or
**UNKNOWN** so you can tell what we have actually shown from what we think.

Generated by `python phase2/scripts/mentor_demo.py`. Nothing here was
hand-picked after the fact — how the scenes were chosen is stated in section 8.

---

## 1. What are we demonstrating?

That our trained model, given two ordinary camera images of the same moment,
produces a **distance estimate for every pixel in the picture** — and that it
really is using both cameras to do it.

## 2. What goes into the system?

Two photographs, taken at the same instant, by two cameras a fixed distance
apart on the same rig. That distance is 53 cm on this dataset (KITTI, a car
with a stereo rig on the roof). Nothing else: no laser scanner, no radar, no
map, no prior knowledge of the scene.

See `01_input/` — for each scene, `..._left.png` and `..._right.png`. They look
almost identical. That is the point.

## 3. What comes out?

A **dense distance map**: one estimated distance for every pixel of the left
image. Not a handful of measured points — the whole picture, 453,376 pixels per
frame.

See `03_depth/` (the raw map) and `04_overlay/` (the map painted over the photo,
which is much easier to read).

## 4. How does it work, at a very high level?

Hold a finger up and blink one eye, then the other. The finger appears to jump
sideways. Something far away barely moves. **How far a thing jumps between the
two views tells you how far away it is** — a big jump means close, a small jump
means far.

The model does exactly that, for every pixel:

```
      TWO CAMERAS            two photos, taken at the same moment
            |
            v
   FIND THE CORRESPONDENCE   for each point in the left photo, find the same
            |                point in the right photo
            v
        DISPARITY            how far sideways it moved, measured in pixels
            |
            v
   ESTIMATED DISTANCE        distance = (focal length x camera separation)
                             divided by that sideways shift
```

Two words appear throughout the pictures and they are **not** the same thing:

| Word | What it is | Units |
|---|---|---|
| **Disparity** | how many *pixels* sideways a point moved between the two photos | px |
| **Depth / distance** | how far away it is in the real world | metres |

Disparity is what the network predicts. Distance is calculated from it using
the camera geometry recorded with the dataset — for these scenes, focal length
718.9 px and camera separation 0.5323 m. **Nothing is invented**: if a scene
had no calibration file, the tool shows disparity only and says on the figure
that metric distance is unavailable.

## 6. Can we point at a region and get a distance?

Yes. `05_probe/` shows it. Four boxes are drawn on every scene — the *same four
boxes on every scene*, fixed in advance, so none was moved to make a number look
good. Each is labelled with the median sideways shift inside it and the distance
that shift implies.

**MEASURED**, scene `000191_10` (a motorway):

| Box | Estimated shift | Estimated distance |
|---|---|---|
| road ahead (bottom of the frame) | 48.4 px | 7.9 m |
| left side | 19.2 px | 19.9 m |
| right side | 18.3 px | 20.9 m |
| far centre (down the road) | 6.6 px | 57.8 m |

Read down the table: the shift shrinks and the distance grows, exactly as the
geometry says it should. The interactive version of this — click any pixel, drag
any box — already exists and is run with:

```
python phase2/scripts/stereo_visualizer.py --scene 31 --models H2 --interactive
```

**Important:** a box is a *region of the image*. The system has **no object
detector**. It does not know that a box contains a car. It estimates the
distance of whatever pixels are inside the box.

## 7. Why are two cameras useful? (the stereo dependence test)

A fair objection: maybe the model ignores the second camera and is just guessing
depth from the left photo alone — the way a person can judge depth from a single
photograph, from experience about how big things usually are.

So we tested it. `06_stereo_test/` keeps the left image **completely unchanged**
and replaces the second camera's image with a photo of a *different road*. If
the model were ignoring the second camera, the output would barely move.

**MEASURED**, scene `000191_10`:

| | normal | second camera replaced |
|---|---|---|
| average disparity error | 1.29 px | 33.25 px |
| pixels off by more than 3 px | 7.7 % | 98.9 % |

The output collapses — the distance map goes nearly uniform, as the picture
shows. **INFERRED:** the model is genuinely matching between the two cameras,
not producing a single-photo depth guess.

This is a **diagnostic**, not something the system does in normal use. The
corrupted picture is a broken input, deliberately fed in to prove a point.

## 8. Which scenes are shown, and were they cherry-picked?

All 40 validation scenes were scored first; the scores for every one of them are
in `manifest.json`. The three demonstration scenes are ranks 3, 9 and 12 of 40 —
the better half, chosen because they are *legible*, not because they are the
best. **The single worst scene of the 40 is included as a failure case.**

| Scene | Role | Rank (of 40) | Average error | Pixels off by > 3 px |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `000191_10` | demonstration (motorway) | 3 | 1.29 px | 7.7 % |
| `000187_10` | demonstration (suburban street) | 9 | 1.32 px | 8.9 % |
| `000160_10` | demonstration | 12 | 1.27 px | 10.3 % |
| `000161_10` | **failure case** | 40 (worst) | 11.36 px | 40.1 % |

These are **representative demonstration scenes, not a statistical benchmark**.
The rank column is there so you can see exactly where each sits.

### The failure case, not hidden

`000161_10` is worth looking at because it fails in an interesting way. The
overlay still *looks* plausible — near car yellow, trees far. The numbers are
the worst of all 40.

**MEASURED:** in that scene the laser measures shifts up to 129 px on the car in
the immediate foreground. Our model never outputs a shift above 81 px, in this
scene or any other. Excluding those very-near pixels, its average error in the
rest of the scene drops from 11.36 px to 4.21 px.

**INFERRED:** the model cannot express objects closer than roughly 4–5 metres,
so a car right in front of the bumper is estimated as much further away than it
is. **A picture that looks plausible is not the same as a picture that is
accurate**, and this scene is the reason we show error maps as well as pretty
overlays.

## 9. What has actually been demonstrated?

**MEASURED**
- The trained model produced every disparity map in `02_disparity/`, from real
  KITTI stereo pairs, at full 1232x368 resolution, with no NaN or infinite
  values anywhere.
- On the three demonstration scenes its average disparity error is 1.27–1.32 px,
  with 7.7–10.3 % of laser-measured pixels off by more than 3 px.
- Across all 40 validation scenes the error rate ranges from 4.7 % to 40.1 %
  (full table in `manifest.json`).
- Replacing the second camera image raises the error rate from 7.7 % to 98.9 %.

**DERIVED**
- Every distance in metres was computed as (focal length x camera separation)
  divided by the estimated shift, using the calibration file shipped with each
  scene. No focal length or baseline was assumed or invented.
- Pixels where the shift is zero, negative or non-finite get no distance at all
  — they are drawn grey and labelled invalid, never as a plausible number.

**INFERRED**
- The overlays suggest the model captures the broad structure of the scene: road
  surface receding, foreground objects separated from background, ordering of
  near and far that matches what the photo shows.
- The stereo-dependence result indicates real correspondence matching rather
  than single-image depth guessing.

**UNKNOWN — not demonstrated by anything here**
- Accuracy in absolute terms. This model is trained on 160 scenes and is a
  research baseline, not a competitive stereo system.
- Anything closer than about 4 metres (see the failure case).
- Live camera operation, frame rate, or real-time performance. **Not measured.**
- Behaviour on any camera, scene type or weather other than KITTI's daytime
  German road footage.
- Deployment on the target accelerator hardware.
- Object detection or recognition of any kind. There is **no detector** in this
  system.
- Suitability for navigation, obstacle avoidance or any safety function.

## In one paragraph

We give the model two images of the same scene, taken at the same instant from
two cameras a fixed distance apart. It finds where each point has shifted
between the two views. That shift tells us how far away the point is. The
visualization turns that into a distance map, so we can see the estimated
distance across the whole scene — and point at any part of it and read the
number off.
